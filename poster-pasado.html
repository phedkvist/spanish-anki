in the past tense:
The whole tense in two ending sets
-ar é  aste  ó  amos  asteis  aron
-er / -ir í  iste  ió  imos  isteis  ieron
Drop the -ar / -er / -ir and add them: hablar → hablé. The stress lands on the ending, which is what makes it sound like the past. The verbs marked orange swap to a short stem and take a third set with no accents: e iste o imos isteis ieron — tuve, never tuvé.
ser and ir share one past tense: fui, fuiste, fue — only the sentence tells you which.  ·  dar and ver take the -er/-ir endings with no accents: di, dio · vi, vio.
haber: hubo = there was / there were.  ·  gustar: me gustó + singular, me gustaron + plural.  ·  creer, leer, oír: i → y in él and ellos (creyó, leyeron, oyó), accent elsewhere (creíste, leímos).  ·  Forms in full orange break their own pattern.
