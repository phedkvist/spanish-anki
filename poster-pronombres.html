The Little Words

which one, and where it goes:

1. Before a noun — articles the / a — they agree with the noun

singularpluralsingularplural
masculineel librolos librosun librounos libros
femininela casalas casasuna casaunas casas
thetheasome
el café · la música · los perros · las llaves
a + el → al (voy al cine) · de + el → del (el coche del profesor)

2. Before the verb — me, te, le, nos, os, les to or for someone — this is the gustar set

meto menosto us
teto youosto you all
leto him / her / you (usted)lesto them
Nos gusta este restaurante.
lit. this restaurant pleases us → we like this restaurant
Nos gustan los restaurantes.
the verb agrees with the thing liked, never with you: one thing → gusta, several → gustan
A mis amigos les gusta la música.
naming the person needs both halves: a + person, and the pronoun still stays · les matches the people, gusta matches la música

3. Before the verb — lo, la, los, las the thing itself: it, him, her, them

memenosus
teyouosyou all
lo / lahim, it (m.) / her, it (f.)los / lasthem (m. / f.)
¿Ves el libro? — Sí, lo veo. = I see it
¿Tienes las llaves? — Sí, las tengo. = I have them
it replaces a noun you already said, and it copies that noun's gender and number

4. Before the verb — me, te, se, nos, os, se back on yourself: reflexive verbs

memyselfnosourselves
teyourselfosyourselves
sehimself / herselfsethemselves
Me levanto a las siete. = I get (myself) up
Se llama Pierre. = he calls himself Pierre = his name is
Nos acostamos tarde. = we go to bed late

5. The same word, two jobs look at what comes next

los libros
los + noun = the books · it's an article
los veo
los + verb = I see them · it's a pronoun

6. Where the little word sits never after the conjugated verb

  • Me gusta el café. · Lo veo. · Se llama Ana. — straight before the verb
  • No me gusta. · No lo veo. — after no, still before the verb
  • Quiero verlo. = Lo quiero ver. — with an infinitive, either stuck on the end or out in front
  • Voy a hacerlo. = Lo voy a hacer. — same with ir a + infinitive
le and les are for the person, lo / la / los / las for the thing: le doy el libro (I give him the book) vs lo doy (I give it).
se also stands in for le / les before lo: se lo doy = I give it to him.
