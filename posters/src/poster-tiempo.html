El Tiempo

four verbs share the job — picking the wrong one is the whole difficulty

1. Which verb English uses it is for all of these; Spanish uses four different things

hace + noun
hace frío, hace sol · the weather does it
está + adjective
está nublado · the state of the sky right now
hay + noun
hay niebla · the thing is simply there
llueve, nieva
one word, no subject · the verb is the weather
There is no rule that predicts which group a word falls into — learn the pair, not the word. hace viento and está nublado and hay niebla all mean “it is …”, and swapping the verbs gives you something no one says.

2. hacer + noun the biggest group, and the one with the trap

  • Hace frío. — it's cold
  • Hace calor. — it's hot
  • Hace sol. — it's sunny
  • Hace viento. — it's windy
  • Hace fresco. — it's chilly, pleasantly cool
  • Hace buen / mal tiempo. — the weather is nice / bad
Everything after hace is a noun, so it takes mucho, never muy:
Hace mucho frío. · Hace mucho calor. · Hace mucho viento.
hace muy frío  hace muy calor

The giveaway: you can count it. un poco de frío, demasiado calor — all noun behaviour.

3. estar + adjective, and hay + noun the sky's state, and what is simply present

  • Está nublado. — it's cloudy
  • Está despejado. — it's clear
  • Está lloviendo. — it's raining, right now
  • Está nevando. — it's snowing, right now
  • Hay niebla. — it's foggy
  • Hay tormenta. — there's a storm
  • Hay nubes. — there are clouds
  • Hay hielo. — it's icy
The ones to unlearn, because English tempts you into them: está ventoso → hace viento  ·  está lluvioso → está lloviendo or llueve  ·  está caluroso → hace calor. Está soleado exists but Spain says hace sol.

4. The verbs with no subject third person singular, and nothing else, ever

llueveit rains / it's rainingllover, o → ue · va a llover = it's going to rain
nievait snows / it's snowingnevar, e → ie · mañana va a nevar
granizait's hailingel granizo = hail
truenait's thunderingel trueno = thunder · el relámpago = lightning
There is no yo for these. yo lluevo is not a mistake you can make in English, which is exactly why it slips out.

5. Degrees, and tomorrow temperature has its own verb again

  • Estamos a veinticinco grados. — it's 25 degrees
  • ¿A cuántos grados estamos? — what's the temperature?
  • Hace veinticinco grados. — also fine, with hacer
  • Estamos a cinco grados bajo cero. — −5 · sobre cero = above
  • La máxima es de 28. — the high · la mínima = the low
  • ¿Qué tiempo hace hoy? — what's the weather like?
  • Va a llover. — it's going to rain
  • Hoy hace más frío que ayer. — colder than
  • En primavera · en verano · en otoño · en invierno — no article

6. Three ways to say “it's cold” and they are not interchangeable

Hace frío.
The weather is cold. Outside, in the world.
Tengo frío.
I am cold. A person has cold in Spanish — never soy or estoy frío.
Está frío.
The thing is cold. Mi café está frío — it wasn't always.
Weather is the clearest case in the language of a meaning that belongs to a pair, not a word. Frío alone tells you nothing: hace frío is the air, tengo frío is you, está frío is your coffee.
